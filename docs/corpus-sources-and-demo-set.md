# Corpus sources, the demo set, and Kyle's manual-download list

**Status as of:** September 18, 2026. Companion to `corpus-catalog.md` (what was researched) and `corpus-missing-originals.md` (every unfetched URL).

**Standing purpose (Kyle, Sept 18).** Every contract, rate card, invoice and framework in this corpus is *data for building the platform*: training and evaluation material for the extraction engine, the normalizer, the reconciliation models and the synthetic-document factory. Only true, sourced, recent-vintage public rates may also seed the benchmark ledger (CLAUDE.md data rules). Old or redacted documents still earn their place because they teach document *structure*.

## 1a. What is inside each set

"Corpus" here means the whole body of real-world documents TWM has collected to build and test the platform before any client data exists. It lives under `corpus/` and has two forms: **originals** (the actual PDFs and web pages as published) and **extractions** (clean tables and notes pulled out of some of them by hand during research).

1. **SEC EDGAR exhibits.** US public companies must file their material contracts with the securities regulator, so their IT outsourcing deals become public record. These are complete legal agreements, often 50 to 300 pages: a master services agreement with numbered articles, then schedules for services, service levels, pricing, governance and exit, then amendments filed over later years. Buyers include banks, insurers, health plans, telecoms and retailers; vendors include IBM, Accenture, TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, CSC and Genpact. Since April 2019 companies may black out pricing, so the older filings are where real rates survive.
2. **UK Contracts Finder.** UK transparency rules require departments to publish the signed contract for large awards. Each is a real executed agreement with order form, statement of work, service levels and schedules, with commercially sensitive numbers redacted. The standout is the Department for Work and Pensions "Synergy" programme, a £710.9M IBM and Oracle deal published as dozens of separate schedule PDFs, which is a complete anatomy of a modern mega-deal. Buyers also include NHS England, the Ministry of Justice, the Cabinet Office and the Department for Education.
3. **Texas DIR contracts.** Texas's central IT agency publishes every document for each of its vendor contracts. A set contains the signed contract, standard terms, a pricing index, a statement of work, a service agreement template and the original request for offer. We hold five sets: four Accenture and one Deloitte. The Accenture application services set includes a pricing exhibit listing about forty roles with onsite and offsite hourly rates for each of eight contract years.
4. **Michigan DTMB contracts.** Michigan publishes each contract as one cumulative PDF: the master contract followed by every change notice and statement of work added over its life. Hourly rates are not redacted. Highlights are the Deloitte "MiIntegrate" contract worth $109M over 13 years with a role, rate and hours table, and the Knowledge Services $800M staff-augmentation programme with a full rate card by job classification. None are on disk yet because the site blocks scripts.
5. **Other US states and federal.** Florida's executed IT staff-augmentation contracts per vendor, across two contract generations. New York's hourly IT services award and its next-generation solicitation package, including the rate bid form and task order forms. A Washington signed work order at $125 an hour, the Oklahoma and Deloitte signed engagement, and two federal master contracts, Alliant 2 and STARS III, which define standard IT labour categories and maximum-rate rules.
6. **CanadaBuys and TBIPS.** Canadian federal solicitations under the Task-Based Informatics Professional Services vehicle, the main way Ottawa buys IT contractors. Each package contains a statement of work annex, a Task Authorization form used to call up individual resources, and a Basis of Payment annex with the per-diem rate tables bidders fill in. Twelve documents are French or bilingual. Awarded rates are not public, so these teach Canadian vocabulary and document shape, not prices.
7. **Invoices, timesheets and audits.** The scarcest material. A Texas utility rate case forced a company to file its staffing vendor's actual invoices with the weekly timesheets behind them, showing names, daily clock times, hours, and regular, overtime and double-time rates. Alongside it are scanned utility vendor invoices from a Kentucky regulator, bankruptcy fee applications from the FTX case with thousands of hours of time entries at named rates, and two government audits that did TWM's reconciliation by hand and listed what they found. Federal invoice and certified payroll templates round it out.
8. **UK G-Cloud 15 rate cards.** The UK government's cloud and digital services framework, renewed August 2026. Every vendor now prices against one standard grid: 58 roles in nine categories, several seniority levels each, with a UK day rate and an offshore day rate. We hold twelve vendors from Accenture to Version 1, about 2,600 priced rows, all dated January 2026. Deloitte prices by Civil Service grade band instead of by role.
9. **UK G-Cloud 14 rate cards.** The previous framework, May 2024. Each vendor published its own PDF rate card using the seven SFIA skill levels, so layouts differ from vendor to vendor. That variety is what makes them useful for extraction training, and comparing them with G-Cloud 15 shows two years of price movement.
10. **US rate schedules.** The Texas staff-augmentation ceiling rates, 60 job titles at six levels, in both the 2020 and 2024 versions. New York's average awarded hourly rates for 31 titles at four levels across three regions, the only source showing what was actually awarded, not a ceiling. Distribution statistics from the federal GSA pricing database for 22 labour categories, and three individual GSA vendor price lists with qualification requirements per category.
11. **Canadian references.** The definitions of roughly 93 TBIPS resource categories and their three experience levels, one full TBIPS solicitation, and Statistics Canada wage data for IT occupations by province. The wage data is what employees earn, not what vendors bill, so it is only a rough proxy.
12. **Skills frameworks.** Published job classification systems. O*NET from the US Department of Labor maps tens of thousands of real job titles to standard occupations. The UK government's digital and data framework defines 53 roles with seniority ladders and skills. Also held: the US cybersecurity workforce framework, European ICT role profiles, and Singapore's ICT career maps. SFIA is deliberately absent pending the licensing decision.

## 2. The demo set — five documents a viewer can follow end to end

Selection rule: the raw document must be public, visually legible, show real money, and between them the five must exercise every stage — ingest → extract → normalize (role, band, tech, location) → ledger/benchmark → reconcile.

**Upgrade when retrieved:** *Michigan DTMB / Deloitte MiIntegrate* (§3, item 1) replaces or joins #3 — a $109M contract with a 13-year change-notice chain and a role × rate × hours table is the best single "follow one contract through time" story in the corpus. **For a Canadian-bank audience** swap in the *SCC TBIPS 2025-03 package* (`corpus/twm_corpus/CA_TBIPS_solicitation_2025-03_structure_notes_ORIGINAL.pdf`) to show Task Authorization + Basis of Payment forms and TBIPS levels.

Demo caveat to keep honest: documents 3–5 are older or non-IT-staffing vintages. They demonstrate the *pipeline*; only documents 1–2 are current enough to show as benchmark evidence.
