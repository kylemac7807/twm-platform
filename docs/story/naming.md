# Naming the business and the application

**Draft for conversation, October 13, 2026.** Options for Kyle to choose from, with the reasoning and the public checks already done. Nothing here is legal clearance; the last section lists the checks that still need a registrar, a trademark database or counsel.

## Where we start

The Business Plan v1.5 says it plainly: "On an interim basis, this organization will be called Technology Workforce Management (TWM). The business will be renamed when further established." The Pitch Deck v1.1 already names the product's parts: the Master Benchmark Ledger, the Role Composition Heatmap, the Total Potential Dashboard. Those are good feature names and can stay whatever the company is called.

## What the name has to do

1. Tell a CIO what the thing is on first hearing, or at least not mislead. The product reads contracts and keeps a book of record of every rate; the promise is that nothing in it is an estimate.
2. Survive the move the Business Plan describes, from consulting-led to software-led, and the later move beyond technology labour to all labour. "Technology Workforce" boxes both in.
3. Stay clear of the names already in the field: Workday, Fieldglass, VNDLY, Ariba, ISG, Gartner, and the mortgage and insurance "Rate-" brands (Ratehub, Rate.com, RateSetter), which crowd the word "rate" for consumers though not for enterprise procurement.
4. Be pronounceable and inoffensive in English and French, because the first clients are Canadian banks.
5. Be registrable as a trademark in Canada and the United States, which favours a distinctive or coined word over a descriptive one.
6. Have a usable domain. Every plain English compound's .com is already registered (23 of 23 checked below), which is normal; the realistic routes are a .ai or .ca domain, buying a parked .com, or a coined word.

## One name or two?

Recommendation: **one company name, and a plain descriptive name for the application** ("the <Company> Ledger", or simply "the Ledger" inside the product). A second coined name for the application costs attention in every meeting, and the deck has already made "ledger" the product's noun. The feature names from the deck stay as they are.

## A shortlist to talk about

1. **Ratetrail.** Clean on every check run; says what the product does in a phrase finance people already use; .ai and .ca are free, and the .com is a purchase conversation with its holder.
2. **Ratewright.** The most distinctive and the easiest to trademark; the price is the spelling on the phone and a recently registered .com.
3. **Quorate.** The best story for the consortium and a real word; the price is proximity to two Quorum software companies and a taken .ai.

For the application in any of the three: "the Ratetrail Ledger", "the Ratewright Ledger", "the Quorate Ledger", with Master Benchmark Ledger kept as the deck's name for the consortium-wide table.

## Checks still to do before choosing

- **Trademarks.** United States: the USPTO trademark search (tmsearch.uspto.gov), classes 9 and 42 (software, software as a service) and 35 (business consulting). Canada: the Canadian Trademarks Database at the Canadian Intellectual Property Office. United Kingdom and EU if the G-Cloud market matters: UKIPO and EUIPO searches. A clearance opinion from counsel on the final two or three.
- **Corporate name.** A NUANS report (the federal and Ontario name search) before incorporating or renaming; the Ontario Business Registry and Corporations Canada for existing registrations.
- **Domains.** Confirm the .ai and .ca at a registrar and register them the same day as the decision; ask the registrar's broker for a quote on the .com (parked domains from 2020 to 2026 are often for sale at four figures).
- **Handles.** LinkedIn company page and the social handles, checked by hand.
- **Language.** A French read by a native speaker for the Quebec banks, and a say-it-out-loud test on the phone with two people who have not seen it written.
- **Reputation.** A plain web search of the name with "scam", "lawsuit" and "review" to catch anything a search for the company alone did not surface.
