Follow one rate: from the page to the ledger

One real number from a signed State of Texas contract with Accenture, followed through the six stages of the pipeline. The same row appears under every stage, exactly as the code recorded it. Beneath it, the row that gets flagged instead, because refusing to guess is the point.

Everything here is real. The document is public and unchanged. Every value is read from the ledger written by the run of October 6, 2026 or from the page itself. Nothing is mocked, rounded or filled in. Where the system did not know, the field is empty and says so.

4

Extraction

A frontier model (rented, reached through one wrapper so it can be swapped) reads the table and fills a typed record: title, rate, unit, currency, period, location, each with a confidence. It records the words as written and does not interpret them. Then the software checks that every number it returned literally appears on the page at the claimed spot.

5

Normalization

Turns the vendor’s words into the standard categories the ledger needs: a canonical role (one of 130) and a seniority band (one of four: junior, intermediate, senior, lead/principal). The rate itself is never changed. A title is resolved once, with its reasoning, and written to a mapping table; every later occurrence is a lookup, so the same title can never resolve two ways.

Coming: savings stacking

This box is a placeholder on purpose. No finding exists yet, and nothing below is computed. Once findings exist, this is where the row’s savings will appear, stacked into three tranches so that no dollar is counted twice:

Contractual

The rate billed against the contracted rate, the price the statement of work or master agreement says the vendor may charge for this role. No contracted rate for the role means $0 here, with the reason stated, and a separate finding that a role was billed without one.

Operational

The contracted rate against the vendor’s own best rate for the same role and band at this client. No second rate from the same vendor means $0 and says so.

Market alignment

Against the market mid-point for the same role, band and location: the consortium-plus-public distribution, published only when a cut has at least 3 observations and 2 contributing clients. A rate already below the mid-point is reported as below market, a fact, never a negative saving.

Observed and projected savings will be two labelled numbers, never one blended total. A missing reference always yields zero plus an explanation, never a guess.

Terms used on this page

Document library
The collection of real documents gathered to build the product. Example: the 13 UK G-Cloud rate cards and this Texas contract.
Contract set
The files that belong to one contract. Example: this master agreement, its statement of work, exhibits and attachments, seven files.
Contracted rate
The price the statement of work or master agreement says the vendor may charge for a role. Example: $83.00 per hour for an Advanced Systems Engineer, onsite, in year 1.
Ledger
The book of record of every rate seen, with its source. Example: row 1 above.
Mapping table
The list of vendor titles already resolved to a role and band, with the reasoning and who decided. Example: “Advanced Systems Engineer” to Systems Engineer, senior.
Role and band
The two axes of the role framework: what the person does (130 roles) and how senior they are (four bands). Location and technology are recorded beside them, never multiplied into the rate.
Box
Four numbers giving a position on a page: left, top, right, bottom, in points from the top-left corner. Example: [65, 128, 553.8, 132.6].
Confidence
A number from 0 to 1 that a stage attaches to its own answer. Below the floor, the answer is withheld and the item is flagged. Example: the model’s 0.30 for “Operations”.
Counted
Whether a row may enter benchmark distributions and savings totals. Only rows above the confidence threshold, from a usable scan, with a resolved title, count.
Model
A frontier language model, rented through one wrapper. TWM owns everything around it: the record shapes, prompts, checks, the role framework, the mapping table and the evaluation sets.
