## Identity

- **Type:** M&A transition services agreement (TSA) covering IT services and non-IT professional services — a distinct contract genus from MSAs/SOWs

## Pricing Model (cost-based TSA)
Overarching principle: "Provider will neither sustain a loss, nor earn a profit" (cost-plus-zero-profit).
| Category | Schedule | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Transition Employees | 5(a)(i) | Loaded cost per employee |
| IT Services | 5(a)(iii) | **Hourly rates** |
| Non-IT Transition Services | 5(a)(iv) | Loaded costs per employee |
| Retained Employees/Contractors | 5(a)(v) | Loaded costs |
| Direct Costs | 5(a)(vi) | Estimated direct costs; pre-approval required above $1,000 |
- Payment: Net 60 from invoice; TSA monthly invoice by 15th of following month

## Key Commercial Terms
- Duration: 18 months from 2004-12-31, extendable; individual services terminable on 30 days' notice
- Service standard: same service levels as performed for the Business immediately prior to Effective Date
- Transition Employees: Provider remains employer, 30-day notice pre-termination, 18-month cap; Retained Employees seconded through 2006-06-30
- Special Projects: separately priced; Provider responds in 5-10 business days; work product owned by Recipient
- Subcontracting: incumbent subcontractors (incl. IBM) continue; new ones need approval
- Disputes: 10-day negotiation → project-manager escalation → AAA arbitration (3 arbitrators with life-reinsurance experience)

## Why useful to TWM
Documents the TSA archetype — cost-plus-zero-profit pricing, loaded-cost vs hourly-rate service categories, seconded/retained employee economics, service-continuity standards — a contract type that appears throughout bank/insurer M&A and carve-outs and differs structurally from vendor MSAs.
